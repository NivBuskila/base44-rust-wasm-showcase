About Aether

Aether began as an experiment in making physics feel tangible: what if a fluid simulation responded to your body as naturally as water responds to a hand trailing through it?

How it works

The core engine is written in Rust. Each frame it advects velocity and dye through a grid, diffuses them, and projects the result so the fluid stays incompressible. Compiled to WebAssembly, the same code runs in a single thread everywhere and, on browsers that allow cross-origin isolation, across several Web Workers in parallel for extra detail.

A separate perception worker runs hand-tracking models locally to find your fingertips and palms, and recognises gestures such as pinches and open hands. Those signals become forces and colour sources inside the simulation. A performance governor watches the frame rate and quietly trades resolution for smoothness when your device needs it.

Privacy

Aether has no server-side processing and no accounts. Your camera images never leave your device, and no tracking data is collected. Close the tab, and everything is gone.
