Paint with light and liquid, using only your hands

Aether is a real-time fluid simulation that lives in your browser tab. Point your webcam at yourself, move your hands, and watch glowing currents of colour swirl, split and merge in response to every gesture you make.

Under the surface, a physics engine written in Rust and compiled to WebAssembly solves the equations of an incompressible fluid many times per second, while on-device hand tracking turns your fingers into brushes, emitters and obstacles. Nothing is uploaded: the camera feed and every calculation stay on your own device, so it is private by design.

What you can do

Stir the field with an open palm, pinch to pour bright dye, or bring both hands together to create vortices that spin off across the screen. No camera? Aether falls back to an ambient simulation you can stir with your mouse or finger. It adapts its quality to your hardware, using multithreading and WebGPU where available, so it stays smooth on laptops and phones alike.
